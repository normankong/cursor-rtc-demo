How to test barge-in

  1. Join the same room in two tabs. Keep both mics unmuted.
  2. One side talks for a few seconds (AEC needs ~1–4s of far-end audio to converge).
  3. The other side interrupts mid-sentence. You should hear the interruption, not a howl.
  4. Flip Echo cancellation off, then on, to compare.

Speakers

This is the real AEC test. Laptop speaker → mic is the echo path. Chrome recommended.

Headphones

Almost no acoustic echo, so barge-in always feels clean. Use them when AEC struggles (Bluetooth, two laptops in one room).

  • Chrome / Edge ship AEC3. Firefox speakerphone is weaker. Safari is usually strong on Apple hardware.
  • Interrupt pauses far-end audio locally (target <200ms) — the software stop voice agents use on TTS. Resume to hear them again. Human barge-in does not require this; AEC + open mic is the real test.
  • Loopback self-test plays your mic back through WebRTC in this tab — AEC on should not ring; AEC off often does on speakers.

Constraints are requests. MDN echoCancellation documents boolean and Chrome 141+ "all" / "remote-only". Trust the HUD’s getSettings() value.
